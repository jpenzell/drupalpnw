### Screen P1.1 — Opening Provocation (15–20 min)

* **Title:** Probabilistic AI & the Efficiency Paradox
* **Purpose:** Set stakes: fluent ≠ true; access ≠ impact.
* **Beats:**

  * LLMs predict likely tokens (probabilistic), not certain truth → treat outputs as **drafts to coach**.
  * **Efficiency paradox:** bolting AI on old workflows can **multiply rework**.
  * **Key study:** RCT of physicians: giving an LLM **did not improve** diagnostic accuracy vs. conventional resources—even though the **LLM alone outperformed** both physician groups on vignettes → tools without workflow/skill change don’t raise performance. ([JAMA Network][1])
* **Micro-demo:** Ask the model a niche policy fact → highlight confident error → “What would break if this were wrong?” (sticky-notes board).
* **Assets:** Sticky-note wall; “risk map” canvas.

---

### Screen P1.2 — AI Fundamentals (10–12 min)

* **Title:** How we got here (plain language)
* **Purpose:** Demystify LLMs for HR leaders.
* **Beats:** rules → ML → LLMs → copilots; **hallucinations**; **prompt sensitivity**; **bias mirrors data**; HIPAA/PHI care in healthcare.
* **Data bite:** For “inside-frontier” knowledge tasks, consultants using GPT-4 delivered **+12.2% tasks, 25.1% faster, ~40% higher quality** in a large HBS/BCG field experiment. ([Harvard Business School][2])

---

### Screen P1.3 — Ethics in Healthcare & HR (10–12 min)

* **Title:** Guardrails that build trust
* **Purpose:** Ground the session in ethical use.
* **Beats:**

  * **GREAT PLEA** principles for **genAI in healthcare** (Governability, Reliability, Equity, Accountability, Traceability, Privacy, Lawfulness, Empathy, Autonomy). ([Nature][3])
  * HR compliance lens: NYC **Local Law 144** (AEDT bias audits) + continuing federal anti-discrimination duties; annual bias audit & notice obligations where applicable. ([New York City Government][4])
* **Asset:** One-screen checklist: **human-in-the-loop**, sensitive data handling, bias checks, transparency.

---

### Screen P1.4 — Mini-Demo: Plain-English Rewrite (5 min)

* **Title:** Turn jargon into human language
* **Purpose:** Show immediate utility + verification habit.
* **Flow:** Paste anonymized clinical/benefits note → “Rewrite for 6th-grade reading level, 120 words, warm tone.” Ask: “What must we verify?” (facts/dates/policy).
* **Note:** Empathy research: Chatbot responses were preferred 4:1 and rated **higher quality and empathy** than physicians’ in a head-to-head text study—use **as first drafts**, then review. ([JAMA Network][5])

#### For each viewpoint below: show a **tabbed screen** with: “Why it matters → Example → Ready-to-run prompt → Quick activity.” (~10–12 min each)

### Screen P1.A — Probabilistic Thinking (10–12 min)

* **Why:** Prevent over-trust; RCT shows access ≠ impact without changed behavior. ([JAMA Network][1])
* **Healthcare-HR example:** Exit-interview text themes → validate.
* **Prompt:** “Extract 5 attrition themes **with confidence notes** + 3 validation questions per theme.”
* **Activity:** Mark what you’d verify before acting.

### Screen P1.B — Conversational Curiosity (10–12 min)

* **Why:** **No universal magic prompt**; small phrasing swings item-level performance—so iterate. ([Wharton Generative AI Labs][6])
* **Example:** Iterate a **return-to-work** memo for clinic staff.
* **Drill:** Round 1 basic draft → Round 2 add role/audience/constraints → Round 3 ask for risks/edge cases.

### Screen P1.C — Reflective Metacognition (10–12 min)

* **Why:** Examine framing; item-level swings are real even if they wash out at scale. ([Wharton Generative AI Labs][6])
* **Example:** Performance-plan language → supportive vs punitive outputs.
* **Prompt overlay:** “If this answer misleads us, what’s the failure path?”

### Screen P1.D — Fluid Identity (10–12 min)

* **Why:** Multi-stakeholder stress-testing in minutes.
* **Mini-demo:** Same scheduling policy → responses as nurse manager, compliance, new grad → adjust original note.

### Screen P1.E — Abundance Curation (10–12 min)

* **Why:** Human curation turns AI volume into quality; performance gains depend on **curation** and **process redesign**. ([Harvard Business School][2])
* **Flash-ideation:** “List 10 **low-risk** AI pilots for Hanger HR (healthcare context).”
* **Vote:** Pick top 2; define success metric + review step.

### Screen P1.F — **Develop & Delegate** (10–12 min)

* **Why:** Move from doing to leading: **develop** the assistant (prompts/workflows/patterns), then **delegate** routine work—review like a junior’s draft.
* **Example:** HR manager designs a CoPilot workflow to draft job posts; manager approves final.
* **Micro-plan:** Each person names **one task** to develop & delegate this week + one coaching behavior (review criteria, tone check).

## Part Two — Stories, live demo, adoption, commitments (≈120 min)

### Screen P2.0 — Bridge (2 min)

* **Title:** From lenses to action
* **Purpose:** We’ll see where **AI is already working** in HR & healthcare, then build live demos.

### Screen P2.1 — Case Card: Clinical Documentation / Scribes (10–12 min)

* **Headline:** Ambient AI scribes (Nuance DAX, Abridge, etc.)
* **Evidence:**

  * Providence randomized study: DAX Copilot users reported **≈2.5 hours/week less** documentation, **−30.3% burnout**, **−49.5% frustration**, **−51.7% self-reported doc time**. ([blog.providence.org][7])
  * Peterson Health Tech Institute review: early evidence shows **burnout reduction**, but **efficiency/financial gains** are mixed so far; evaluate locally. ([Peterson Health Technology Institute][8])
* **Implication for HR:** Well-being & retention lever; guardrails & evaluation design matter.

### Screen P2.2 — Case Card: Scheduling & Ops (6–8 min)

* **Headline:** AI for nurse scheduling
* **Evidence:** Providence reports **~95% reduction in scheduling time** while maintaining fairness/trust (case narrative). ([hrhealthcare.wbresearch.com][9])
* **Use it to discuss:** process redesign + trust; monitoring for fairness.

### Screen P2.3 — Case Card: HR Service & Comms (6–8 min)

* **Headline:** HR chat assistants / employee comms
* **Evidence pointer:** Chatbot empathy/quality studies support first-draft uses for sensitive comms (with human review). ([JAMA Network][5])
* **Quick prompt:** “Draft benefits FAQ answer at 6th-grade level; cite policy clause numbers.”

### Screen P2.4 — Case Card: Talent & Compliance (8–10 min)

* **Headline:** AI in hiring under scrutiny
* **Compliance:** NYC **Local Law 144** → bias audits & notices; continuing federal anti-discrimination duties (Title VII, ADEA, ADA). ([New York City Government][4])
* **Live context:** Ongoing litigation spotlighting AI hiring tools (e.g., Workday case coverage). ([Reuters][10])
* **Takeaway:** Use AI for **assistive screening**, not final decisions; require audits, transparency, human review.

---

### Screen P2.5 — Working Demo: “You pick, we build” (25–30 min)

* **Purpose:** Real-time problem-solving with CoPilot/LLM using **audience use cases**.
* **Demo menu (use 2–3):**

  1. **Policy drafting:** “Draft a 300-word RTO update for clinic staff; friendly, clear, includes appeal path.”
  2. **Data scan:** “Analyze anonymized attrition table; chart top 3 drivers; list 5 follow-up questions.”
  3. **Patient/employee comms:** “Rewrite discharge instructions (or benefits change) for 6th-grade level.”
* **Pattern to model:** prompt → output → **iterate** (add role/audience/constraints) → **verify** (call out checks).
* **Annotations:** On-screen “Prism tags” (e.g., you just used **B: Conversational Curiosity**, **E: Abundance Curation**).
* **Caution card:** Accuracy/PHI rules; use anonymized inputs.

### Screen P2.6 — Leadership & Adoption (20–25 min)

* **Title:** Empowering adoption & planning ahead
* **Beats:**

  * **Leadership, Lab, Crowd**: Individual gains don’t auto-become org gains—leaders sponsor, create sandboxes, invite broad participation. ([oneusefulthing.org][11])
  * HBS/BCG **Jagged Frontier**: know where AI helps vs. harms; redesign workflows around **inside-frontier** tasks. ([Harvard Business School][2])
  * Practical enablers: micro-training, prompt libraries, champions, success stories, **guardrails** (GREAT PLEA + AEDT compliance). ([Nature][3])
* **Mini-activity (5 min):** Identify one **pilot** (scope, metric, owner, risk check).

* **Close:** You are **AI leaders**—coach the assistant, protect people, redesign the work.

## Live Prompts (ready to paste on demo screens)

1. **Plain-English rewrite**
   “Rewrite the text below at a 6th-grade reading level (≤120 words), warm & clear. Keep all factual details and dates. After the rewrite, list 3 things a human should verify before sending.”

2. **Attrition scan**
   “You are an HR analyst. From the attached (anonymized) table, chart attrition by department and tenure band. Then list the top 3 hypotheses and 5 follow-up questions to validate causes. Include a 3-bullet ‘what to do next.’”

3. **Policy draft**
   “Draft a 300-word return-to-work update for clinic staff. Friendly, specific, includes (1) reasoning, (2) timeline, (3) escalation/appeal path, (4) link placeholders for FAQs. Tone: respectful, supportive. End with 3 ‘If you’re affected’ next steps.”

4. **Prism iteration overlay**
   “Re-draft for (a) nurse manager, (b) compliance, (c) new grad. Summarize the top risk/concern each would raise.”
